## General Duties of the Occupier

The occupier of a factory has a duty, so far as is reasonably practicable, to ensure
the health, safety and welfare of all workers while they are at work.

This duty extends to the provision and maintenance of plant and systems of work that
are safe and without risk to health; arrangements for the safe use, handling, storage
and transport of articles and substances; the provision of information, instruction,
training and supervision necessary to ensure health and safety; the maintenance of
safe places of work with safe means of access and egress; and the provision and
maintenance of an adequate working environment.

The occupier is required to prepare a written statement of general policy with
respect to the health and safety of workers, and to bring it to their notice.

## Chapter IV-A — Provisions Relating to Hazardous Processes

Chapter IV-A applies to factories carrying on a hazardous process — a process or
activity where, unless special care is taken, the raw materials, intermediates or
finished products would cause material impairment to the health of persons engaged,
or result in pollution of the general environment.

The chapter establishes site appraisal committees, compulsory disclosure of
information, specific responsibilities of the occupier, medical examinations,
permissible limits of exposure, workers' participation in safety management, and the
right of workers to warn about imminent danger.

## Compulsory Disclosure of Information

The occupier of a factory involving a hazardous process must disclose all information
regarding the dangers of the process — including health hazards and the measures to
overcome them — to the workers employed, to the Chief Inspector, to the local
authority, and to the general public in the vicinity.

Before beginning a hazardous process, the occupier must inform the Chief Inspector of
the nature and details of the process, in the prescribed manner. Information must not
be withheld on grounds of commercial confidentiality where it bears on worker safety.

## Specific Responsibility of the Occupier in Hazardous Processes

The occupier must maintain accurate and up-to-date health records of workers exposed
to hazardous substances, and must appoint persons with the qualifications and
experience necessary to supervise the handling of hazardous substances.

The occupier must provide for medical examination of every worker before employment
in a hazardous process and at prescribed intervals during employment.

## Permissible Limits of Exposure

Exposure of workers to chemical and toxic substances must not exceed the permissible
limits prescribed by the Act. Where the limits are exceeded, the occupier must take
immediate remedial action and notify the Inspector.

Monitoring exposure is a continuing obligation, not a periodic audit. Measurement
must reflect the concentration to which workers are actually exposed at the work
position, rather than a value convenient to measure.

## Workers' Participation in Safety Management

In every factory carrying on a hazardous process, a safety committee must be
constituted with equal representation of workers and management, to promote
cooperation between them in maintaining proper safety and health at work.

The safety committee reviews the measures taken and is entitled to information
relevant to those measures.

## Right of Workers to Warn About Imminent Danger

Where workers have reasonable apprehension that there is a likelihood of imminent
danger to their lives or health, they may bring it to the notice of the occupier,
agent, manager or other responsible person, directly or through their representatives
in the safety committee, and simultaneously to the Inspector.

The person notified is required to take immediate remedial action if satisfied of the
existence of the danger, and to send a report to the Inspector. Where that person is
not satisfied that a danger exists, the matter is referred to the Inspector, whose
decision is final.

This provision matters for automated safety systems: a system that detects a
developing hazard is a means by which apprehension of imminent danger arises, and the
obligation to act on it does not depend on the alert originating from a person.

## Notice of Certain Accidents and Dangerous Occurrences

Where an accident occurs in a factory causing death or bodily injury preventing a
person from working for a prescribed period, or where a dangerous occurrence of a
prescribed class takes place, the manager must send notice to the prescribed
authorities in the prescribed form and within the prescribed time.

Records of such accidents and dangerous occurrences must be maintained and made
available for inspection. A dangerous occurrence is reportable whether or not it
resulted in injury — a near miss that could have caused serious harm carries the same
reporting obligation.
